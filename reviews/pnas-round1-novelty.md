# PNAS draft — Round 1 review: novelty positioning & citation correctness

**Target:** `papers/markowitz-of-cost-pnas.md`
**Reviewer scope:** (1) novelty vs FSS2022, (2) AJN / AJNT attribution, (3) verifiability of arXiv:2512.12111, (4) CRONE / Oustaloup attribution, (5) reference formatting, (6) "first crossing" claim.

## Review

### Correct

- **arXiv:2512.12111 is real.** `curl https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12111` returns HTTP 200 with `<title>[2512.12111] Fractional Calculus in Optimal Control and Game Theory: Theory, Numerics, and Applications -- A Survey</title>`. Authors: Mojahed N., Fatoorehchi H., Nazari S.; submission date 2025-12-13. Abstract covers Caputo / RL / Grünwald–Letnikov, Oustaloup, fractional Pontryagin / HJB / HJI / LQR / MPC across "physical, biological, and engineered systems" — no mention of optimal execution, liquidation, market impact, propagator, or Almgren. The claim in §4.6 that this survey "does not cover optimal execution" is supportable from the abstract alone. Also consistent with the more detailed audit trail in `outputs/bulk-fractional-forecast-novelty.md` §3.2 and `.provenance.md` lines 62–63.
- **FSS2022 attribution is fair and internally consistent.** §1 final paragraph and §4.6 both credit FSS2022 Theorem 2.2 for the half-order Riemann–Liouville content on the bounded interval ($T = B^{-1}I_\nu B$, $r=(1-\gamma)/2$), and explicitly identify what is new (whole-line stationary formulation, forecast curve as the operated-on object, Markowitz correspondence, temporal-whitening reading). This is consistent with the technical paper's §1.3 "closest prior art" paragraph and Remark 4.1.1, and with the 2026-06-28 novelty-audit CHANGELOG entry, both of which say the structural fractional-operator insight is *already implicit in FSS2022* and that novelty is presentation + unification + cross-field bridge.
- **CRONE / Oustaloup attribution.** Ref [15] correctly cites Oustaloup's 1991 Hermès monograph (original French title, correct publisher). Ref [16] Chen–Petráš–Xue 2009 ACC tutorial is a standard entry point to fractional-order control and is real. The description in §4.6 ("fractional-order controllers with power-law memory constitute the CRONE tradition") is accurate.
- **Reference list generally clean.** Spot-checks pass for refs [1] Markowitz 1952 J. Finance 7:77–91, [3] Bouchaud–Gefen–Potters–Wyart 2004 Quant. Finance 4:176–190, [5] Jusselin–Rosenbaum 2020 Math. Finance 30:1309–1336, [12] FSS2022 Quant. Finance 22:585–596, [14] AJNT arXiv:2403.10273, [17] Arveson 1975 JFA 20:208–233, [22] Gatheral–Schied–Slynko 2012 Math. Finance 22:445–474. Söhngen 1939 Math. Z. 45:245–264 and Tricomi 1957 Interscience are cited correctly.
- **"First crossing" claim survives scrutiny.** Combining (i) verified fact that the Dec 2025 fractional-control survey does not mention execution, (ii) the parent project's own literature audit (`outputs/bulk-fractional-forecast-novelty.md` §3, `outputs/fractional-kernels-optimal-execution.md`), and (iii) the fact that FSS2022, AJN, AJNT, GSS, GP, and other execution papers do not cite Oustaloup / CRONE, the claim that this is the first execution↔CRONE crossing is defensible. The paper hedges appropriately ("has not previously been connected", not "is provably first").

### Blocker — none

No fabricated references. No unsupported novelty claim that would sink the paper.

### Fix worth doing now

- **Ref [23] is malformed.** Missing authors and the full title is truncated. Actual arXiv metadata:
  > Mojahed N, Fatoorehchi H, Nazari S (2025) Fractional calculus in optimal control and game theory: Theory, numerics, and applications — A survey. arXiv:2512.12111.
  The current entry "23. Fractional calculus in optimal control and game theory: A survey. arXiv:2512.12111 (2025)." would fail a copy-editor pass at PNAS.
- **Significance Statement and Abstract are more assertive of novelty than §1 and §4.6.** The Significance Statement says "We show that … the analogous canonical map is a fractional derivative of order $1-\gamma$" with no acknowledgment that the underlying half-order factorization is in FSS2022. The Abstract likewise. Only in §1's final paragraph is FSS2022 credited. This mismatch is what a hostile referee (plausibly Forde or a coauthor) will target. Recommend one clause in either the Sig Statement or the Abstract framing the contribution as the Markowitz correspondence / temporal-whitening reading, with the half-order factorization credited as classical. The technical companion paper's §1.2 item 1 already does this softening; PNAS §1 does; Sig/Abstract lag behind and should be brought into line with the project's own novelty audit (CHANGELOG 2026-06-28 afternoon).
- **Body-text year for ref [13] is absent, but ref list gives (2025).** Ref [13] "Abi Jaber E, Neuman E (2025) Optimal liquidation with signals: The general propagator case. *Math. Finance* (arXiv:2211.00447)" is fine as arXiv/in-press citation; if Math. Finance has now assigned volume/pages these should be added. If still in-press, prefer explicit "(2025, in press)" or drop the year and leave the arXiv ID.

### Note

- The §1 disclosure "The operator-resolvent framework of Abi Jaber–Neuman (13) and Abi Jaber–Neuman–Tuschmann (14) subsumes the whole-line problem" is slightly generous. The AJN/AJNT resolvent framework is general enough to *cover* the whole-line power-law case, but their setup is not translation-invariant (matrix Volterra with terminal-inventory constraints on finite intervals / half-lines) and does not reduce to the explicit Fourier multiplier form except under the specializations the companion paper §5.3 lists (scalar power-law, stationary, constant temporary impact). Not a novelty problem, but a referee from the AJN camp will notice. Consider "subsumes the propagator model at maximal generality; the closed-form fractional derivative (12) is its scalar power-law, stationary specialization" — which is in fact already the second sentence of §4.6, so this is just a matter of moving the qualifier into the §1 paragraph as well.
- The Hytönen–van Neerven–Veraar–Weis citation "(19, Prop. 2.6.13)" for commutation of conditional expectation with a bounded deterministic operator is in the correct chapter (Ch. 2 of Vol. I, Bochner spaces / conditional expectations) but I did not verify the exact proposition number against the physical book. Worth a quick check before submission; if the number is off by one, the claim itself is standard and any nearby proposition suffices.
- Style-guide adjacency (out of scope but flagged): AGENTS.md forbids "canonical" as an empty intensifier. It appears in the Sig Statement ("canonical solution", "canonical map"), Abstract ("empirically canonical power-law kernel"), and §1 ("canonical starting point"). None affects novelty positioning, but if the intent is to submit to PNAS the abstract/significance will be re-read many times and each instance loses a word for a claim.
- Numerics-availability sentence ("Reference implementations … are available in the companion repository") is standard, but PNAS asks for a concrete data-availability statement with a repository URL or DOI. Fill in before submission.

## Summary

No blockers. arXiv:2512.12111 exists and matches the description. FSS2022, AJN, AJNT, and CRONE attributions are fair and consistent with the parent project's own novelty audit and technical companion. The only concrete fixes worth applying now are (a) complete the malformed ref [23] with authors and full title, and (b) bring the Significance Statement / Abstract into line with the more careful §1 / §4.6 framing so that the FSS2022 half-order factorization is acknowledged in the front matter, not only in the intro's last paragraph and §4.6.
