## FATAL

### F1. Sign and term inconsistency between FOC (★) [§2.4, line ~120] and the stochastic FOC actually inverted in Appendix A.1 (~line 320)

§2.4 states
```
∫_0^T G(|t-v|) u*_v dv  =  λ − E_t[α_T] + α_t            (★)
```
Appendix A.1 writes the same first-order condition as
```
∫_0^T G(|t-v|) u*_v dv  =  α_t − λ          P-a.s.
```
These differ in **sign of λ** and in the **presence/absence** of the
`−E_t[α_T]` term. Theorem 4.1 (§4.2, ~line 240) then takes
`u*_t = κ_γ · D^γ[ s ↦ λ − bar_α(t,s) ](t) + B_γ`, which is sign-consistent
with (★) and sign-*inconsistent* with A.1.

Because the sign of u* flips with the sign convention on the RHS, the
whole policy reverses direction depending on which version of the FOC
the reader follows. The body and appendix must be reconciled before any
of the downstream Riesz/Sonine algebra can be evaluated.

### F2. `E_t[α_T]` in (★) is identically zero by the §2.2 definition of α

§2.2 (~line 100) defines `α_t := E_t[P_T − P_t]`. Therefore
`α_T = E_T[P_T − P_T] = 0` (deterministically), so `E_t[α_T] = 0` for
every t. The term `−E_t[α_T]` that appears in (★), Theorem 5.1
(~line 295), and Theorem 6.1 (~line 460) is either dead notation or a
sign of a mis-derived FOC. If the intended object is the *expected
total drift* `E_t[P_T] − P_0` (or similar non-zero quantity), then the
definition of α in §2.2 needs to be amended; otherwise the term should
be deleted everywhere and the derivation of (★) shown carefully.

These two items (F1, F2) are pre-requisites for the rest of the review:
no amount of Sonine/Riesz manipulation can be checked until the right
equation is being inverted.

## MAJOR

### M1. Theorem 4.1's claim of `F_t`-measurability is correct in principle, but A.1 silently assumes time-consistency of the family `{v_t(·)}_t` (~line 333)

Step (i) of the proof projects (★) onto `F_t` and obtains a *deterministic*
Fredholm equation parameterised by t, then sets `u*_t := v_t(t)` where
`v_t(·)` solves the s-equation. The issue is that the existence of a
single adapted process `u*` whose conditional expectations realise the
whole family of curves — i.e. `E_t[u*_s] = v_t(s)` for all `s > t` —
is a *non-trivial time-consistency requirement*. Solving the Fredholm
at each `t` independently produces a family of curves that is not
automatically generated by a single adapted process.

Abi Jaber–Neuman 2022 (arXiv:2211.00447) handle this by working with
the full stochastic Fredholm equation and using a martingale-representation
step; A.1's invocation of "Abi Jaber–Neuman 2022 Lemma 3.2" as if it
gave a one-line reduction to a deterministic equation **overclaims**
what AJN actually prove. In particular AJN's framework genuinely
involves both adapted-past and conditional-forecast terms simultaneously,
not a clean "project then invert deterministically" split. The hand-waved
flag in A.1 acknowledges this but should be promoted to an explicit
open verification step in the proof sketch.

### M2. Theorems 5.1 and 6.1 are stated with `α_s` / `α_t` instead of the forecast curve, breaking adaptedness (lines ~290–296, ~458–460)

Theorem 4.1 explicitly carries `bar_α(t,s)` so that the operator argument
is `F_t`-measurable. Theorem 5.1 then writes

```
u*_t = ∫_0^T R_{γ,η}(t,s) ( λ − E_s[α_T] + α_s ) ds,
```

with `α_s` under the integral at points `s > t`. Because the Mittag–Leffler
resolvent is non-causal (it spreads mass on both sides of s = t), this
integrand requires the realised future of α to be known at time t. With
the integrand as written, `u*_t` is **not** `F_t`-measurable, and
Theorem 5.1 is therefore not an admissible policy as stated. The same
issue is repeated in Theorem 6.1. Both statements must use
`bar_α(t,s)` (or, equivalently, replace `α_s` by `E_t[α_s]` in the
integrand) — consistent with §4.1.

### M3. §5.4 Wiener–Hopf section conflates execution with stationary-signal portfolio optimisation (lines ~205–260, then ~360–420)

Dropping `X_T = 0` and taking `T → ∞` is **not** an "infinite-horizon
execution problem". Execution is defined by a finite inventory `X_0`
that must be unwound; removing the terminal constraint converts the
problem into a stationary-signal trading problem of the
Gârleanu–Pedersen (2013) type, where well-posedness requires either a
running quadratic inventory penalty `γ X_t²` or some other regulariser
to keep `u*` in `L^2`. None of this is added to the cost functional in
§5.4. Specific symptoms:

- The Lagrange multiplier λ enforces the budget constraint in §2; once
  the constraint is dropped, the role of λ in `(★_WH)` is undefined,
  yet the RHS still carries `α_t − E_t[α_∞]` with no derivation.
- For α stationary and square-integrable (not decaying), there is no
  reason for `u*` to be integrable on `[0,∞)` and the symmetric power-law
  convolution on the LHS of `(★_WH)` need not converge.
- The "execution" label leaks downstream into Corollary 5.3 and the
  "CRONE-2 robust controller for the propagator plant" remark, which
  reads as if the W–H factorisation produced an execution policy.

**Recommendation.** Either (a) honestly relabel §5.4 as the stationary
signal-tracking analogue (Gârleanu–Pedersen setting, with an explicit
risk penalty added to the cost) and present the W–H factorisation as a
mathematical companion to Theorem 4.1, or (b) drop the section. As
currently written it misrepresents scope.

### M4. A.3 "recovery of Forde et al. 2022" uses the wrong fractional-operator semigroup (lines ~395–420)

The argument writes
`D^γ_{[0,T]} ∘ I^{H+1/2}` and invokes
`D^ν I^μ = I^{μ-ν}` "by the Riemann–Liouville semigroup property". But
`D^γ_{[0,T]}` is the **symmetric Riesz operator on a bounded interval**
(per §3.2), whereas the semigroup identity `D^ν I^μ = I^{μ-ν}` is a
property of **same-sided left-RL operators on the half-line**. The two
do not compose by the cited rule:
- on `[0,T]`, the symmetric operator carries boundary weights
  `(s(T-s))^{∓ν}` (per Chakrabarti–George in A.2) which break the
  half-line semigroup;
- the Forde et al. policy involves explicit incomplete-Beta kernels
  precisely because of these boundary weights.

The conclusion that the composition "reduces to a single
Riemann–Liouville operator with kernel of incomplete-Beta type" and
"is exactly Forde et al. (2022) eq. (26)" therefore does **not** follow
from the argument given. This is one of the paper's headline recovery
claims (cited as Corollary 4.3) and presently rests on a heuristic. It
needs either (a) a real computation matching the Forde kernel
coefficient-by-coefficient, or (b) a downgrade to "conjectural".

Unverified externally: I could not pull Forde–Sánchez-Betancourt–Smith
2022 eq. (26) in this shell (no `alpha_*` tools); parent should fetch
the arXiv version and check.

### M5. Remark 4.1.2's claim that AJN 2022's "forward representation = the projection step" is an overclaim (lines ~265–270)

AJN 2022 (arXiv:2211.00447) is set up for a **general** propagator G,
not just power-law, and their main result is a forward-backward
representation that combines:
- the conditional-forecast of the signal, **and**
- a propagator-specific resolvent acting on the adapted past trading
  rate.

The "projection step" of §4.1 in this paper is at most the first half
of AJN's construction; it does not by itself produce AJN's optimal
policy, which requires the resolvent inversion that is power-law–specific
in this draft's Theorem 4.1 but kernel-general in AJN. The remark as
written conflates "common first step" with "common root of both
literatures". Suggest narrowing the claim to: *the projection step of
§4.1 specialises AJN's conditional-forecast construction to the
power-law kernel; AJN's resolvent step is then replaced here by the
explicit Riesz inversion.*

## MINOR

### m1. §3.2 "Sonine pair property" is misstated (lines ~165–170)

The identity
`(I^ν_+ I^{1−ν}_- f)(t) = ∫_0^T k_γ(t,s) f(s) ds`
is not a Sonine-pair statement in the classical sense. Sonine pairs
concern two kernels `k₁`, `k₂` with `(k₁ * k₂)(t) = 1`. The mixed
left/right RL composition produces some explicit kernel, but the kernel
should be named and its connection to Chakrabarti–George spelled out,
not labelled as the Sonine property.

### m2. §5.3 attribution for multi-exponential approximation (~line 320)

"the multi-exponential approximation of the power-law kernel (Abi Jaber–Bondi
et al. 2025)" — arXiv:2503.04323 ("Fredholm Approach to Nonlinear
Propagator Models") is not the canonical reference for the
multi-exponential approximation of fractional kernels. That role is
usually played by Abi Jaber–El Euch (2019, *Math. Finance*, "Multifactor
approximation of rough volatility models") or Abi Jaber (2019, "Lifting
the Heston model"). Re-attribute.

### m3. Citing Cartea–Jaimungal 2016 for the "projection trick" (~line 220)

CJ 2016 use HJB / DPE rather than a Fredholm projection. The lineage
of the projection/forecast-curve construction is closer to Abi Jaber
and co-authors than to CJ. Drop or qualify the citation.

### m4. §2.2 description of α (~line 95)

The prose says α represents *the expected drift of P_t over [t,T]* but
the formula `α_t := E_t[P_T − P_t]` is a cumulative quantity (price
units), not a drift rate (price/time units). This terminology mismatch
is also the proximate cause of F2: a reader implementing α as a
drift-rate process would never see `α_T = 0`.

### m5. §1.1 attribution to Jusselin–Rosenbaum 2020 (~line 50)

"Jusselin–Rosenbaum (2020) prove that power-law impact is the unique
kernel compatible with no-arbitrage and rough volatility" — JR's result
is conditional on a specific microstructure setup and "uniqueness" is
within a parametric class, not unconditional. Slightly overstated.

### m6. Theorem 4.1 boundary-term coefficient `c₁` (~line 245)

`B_γ(t) = c₁(t(T-t))^{(1−γ)/2 − 1}` — the dependence of c₁ on (X₀, λ)
should be stated; as written it reads as a free constant. Also, the
exponent `(1−γ)/2 − 1 = −(1+γ)/2 ∈ (−1, −1/2)` is integrable on (0,T)
but the U-shape is *singular* at the endpoints; worth a sentence on
how this matches GSS's actually-integrable U-solution.

## Summary

- **Sign convention is broken** between body §2.4 and Appendix A.1 (F1)
  and contains a structurally-zero term (F2). Fix these first.
- The headline **recovery results need work**: A.3 (Forde) uses a
  semigroup identity that doesn't apply to the symmetric Riesz operator
  on a bounded interval (M4); Remark 4.1.2 overclaims AJN 2022 (M5).
- The **W–H half-line section** (§5.4) is mis-scoped as execution; it
  is a stationary-signal / GP-style problem that needs a different
  cost functional or honest relabelling (M3).
- Theorems 5.1 and 6.1 must use the **forecast curve** to be admissible
  (M2).
- The §4.1 forecast-curve construction itself is fine, but A.1's
  reduction silently invokes a **time-consistency property** that AJN
  prove with non-trivial machinery, not in a single Lemma 3.2 line
  (M1).

What is sound:
- The §4.1 definition of `bar_α(t,s)` is `F_t`-measurable as claimed
  and is the right object for a non-causal operator acting on the
  conditional forecast.
- The Mittag–Leffler structure of Theorem 5.1's resolvent kernel
  (modulo M2) is the expected form for a second-kind Fredholm with
  power-law convolution.
- The §5.3 statement that exponential resilience is **not** the γ → 1⁻
  limit of the power-law kernel is correct (cross-checks against
  Neuman–Voß 2020 and the multi-exponential bridge discussed in
  Abi Jaber–El Euch 2019 / Abi Jaber–Bondi et al. 2025).
- The Corollary 4.2 zero-signal recovery of the GSS U-shape is
  structurally correct (the Riesz derivative of a constant on a bounded
  interval vanishes in the interior; only the homogeneous boundary term
  carries the inventory).
