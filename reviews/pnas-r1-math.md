# PNAS r1 — mathematical / technical review

Scope: `papers/markowitz-of-cost-pnas.md`; cross-checked against `papers/markowitz-of-cost.md` (long form). No edits made.

## (a) Correctness blockers

None. All headline equations (5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15) are internally consistent and match the long-form derivation. The constant $\kappa_{1-\gamma} = [2c\Gamma(1-\gamma)\sin(\pi\gamma/2)]^{-1} = c_\gamma^{-1}$ (§1.3(iii), eq 12) agrees with the long-form's boxed (FD) at line 325 of `markowitz-of-cost.md`.

## (b) Correctness concerns worth clarifying (not blockers)

1. **§4.1 — "one constant per scalar constraint" mis-states the KKT form.** The paragraph writes
   $$\alpha^{\rm eff}_t = \alpha_t + \sum_k \lambda_k\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,T]}(t)$$
   and lists two example constraints: terminal inventory $\int_0^T u\,dt=-X_0$ and cash neutrality $\int_0^T (T-t)u\,dt=0$. Only the first has adjoint $\mathbf{1}_{[0,T]}$; the second contributes $\lambda(T-t)\mathbf{1}_{[0,T]}(t)$, not a constant. The formula therefore mixes basis vectors: it should be $\alpha^{\rm eff}_t = \alpha_t + \sum_k \lambda_k \phi_k(t)$ where $\phi_k$ is the constraint kernel (or restrict the sum to inventory-type constraints only). Text and formula are inconsistent.

2. **§4.1 — "applying $D_\pm^\beta$ to $\mathbf{1}_{[0,T]}$ produces the two Söhngen–Tricomi modes" is loose.** The Söhngen–Tricomi modes $\phi_1(t)=(t(T-t))^{-\beta}$, $\phi_2(t)=\tfrac{T-2t}{2}\phi_1(t)$ have interior singularities of order $\beta$ at each endpoint, but $\mathbb R$-Marchaud $D_\pm^\beta$ applied to $\mathbf{1}_{[0,T]}$ produces boundary-layer singularities of order $2\beta = 1-\gamma$ (via the Riesz-symbol identity), which is a different exponent, and the result is not supported on $[0,T]$. The correct statement is that the two modes span the homogeneous nullspace of the *finite-interval* Fredholm operator (Söhngen 1939 / Tricomi 1957) and appear when one solves the constant-RHS interval problem — equivalently, via a finite-interval Wiener–Hopf / half-order factorization on $[0,T]$ as in Forde–Sánchez-Betancourt–Smith (ref 10), not by direct application of the whole-line Marchaud operator to an indicator. The paragraph should say "the finite-interval analog of (11) applied to a constant RHS" rather than "applying $D_\pm^\beta$ to $\mathbf{1}_{[0,T]}$". The exponent $\beta$ vs $2\beta$ mismatch is the tell.

3. **§2.6 Step 2 — "commutes with the deterministic bounded operator $D_-^\beta$".** $D_-^\beta$ is not bounded on $L^2(\mathbb R)$; it is bounded $\dot H^\beta \to L^2$. The commutation with conditional expectation is fine (it extends by density to closed linear operators, and the Hytönen ref 22 Prop 2.6.13 is invoked correctly on the appropriate Sobolev domain), but the descriptor "bounded" should either be dropped or qualified as "bounded on the domain $\dot H^\beta$".

4. **§2.4 Lemma 1 statement vs §5 proof.** The lemma assumes $C_+^\ast = C_-$; this is true for the Marchaud/Riemann–Liouville pair $I_\pm^\beta$ and is used implicitly to derive the adjoint triangularity $P_+ C_- P_+^\perp = 0$ from $P_+^\perp C_+ P_+ = 0$. The §5 proof invokes "adjoint statement, using $C_-=C_+^\ast$" — worth stating this hypothesis explicitly in the Lemma (currently only in §2.3 via eq 9). Minor exposition point.

5. **§5, Theorem 1 proof Step (c).** The identity $\hat G(\xi)(i\xi)^\beta = c_\gamma(-i\xi)^{-\beta}$ uses $|\xi|^{-2\beta} = (i\xi)^{-\beta}(-i\xi)^{-\beta}$ which is the standard principal-branch factorization; verified. The subsequent claim $G*D_+^\beta = c_\gamma I_-^\beta$ on $L^2(\mathbb R)$ is only formally correct — both sides are unbounded, and the equation should be understood on $\dot H^\beta$ or on Schwartz. Same Sobolev-domain caveat as concern 3. Not wrong, just under-qualified.

6. **§1.1 dual-norm analogy (eq 3–4).** The Legendre transform value $\tfrac12\|\alpha\|_{C^{-1}}^2$ is correctly identified with the Markowitz Sharpe value $\tfrac{1}{2\lambda}\mu^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mu$ under the correspondence $\lambda\leftrightarrow 1$ (established in §1.1 correspondence line). This is literally right *given* $\lambda=1$ in the gain–cost problem (which is a normalization choice — one can absorb a risk-aversion prefactor into $C$). Reader may be helped by one sentence noting that $\lambda=1$ is a normalization; the choice is implicit in writing (1) with coefficient $\tfrac12$ on the quadratic term.

7. **§2.1 spectral hypothesis.** $\int(1+|\xi|^{2(1-\gamma)+\epsilon})S_\alpha(\xi)\,d\xi<\infty$ ensures $\alpha\in \dot H^{1-\gamma}$ almost surely and hence $u^\star\in L^2$ (used in "Admissibility" at end of §5). The hypothesis is stronger than needed for the mere existence of $u^\star$ in $L^2_{\rm adap}$ (one only needs $\alpha\in\dot H^{(1-\gamma)/2 - \delta}$ or so for the FOC to be well-posed); worth a line noting this bound is what makes the fractional-derivative expression pointwise defined. Minor.

## (c) Items that look fine

- **Wiener–Hopf identity (eq 5, 11) and its proof (§5).** The two triangularity identities $P_+^\perp C_+ P_+ = 0$ (from $C_+$ being causal and hence preserving $L^2_{\rm adap}$) and $P_+ C_- P_+^\perp = 0$ (adjoint) are used correctly. Collapse: for adapted $u$, letting $v=C_+ u$ (adapted), $P_+ C_-^{-1} P_+ C_- v = v$ because $P_+ C_-^{-1} = P_+ C_-^{-1} P_+$ (from $C_-$ preserving the anticausal subspace $P_+^\perp L^2$), so $C_+^{-1}P_+ C_-^{-1}(P_+ C P_+)u = C_+^{-1} v = u$. Verified.
- **Fourier constant $c_\gamma = 2c\Gamma(1-\gamma)\sin(\pi\gamma/2)$** (§2.3, long-form line 299): $\int_{-\infty}^\infty|t|^{-\gamma}e^{-i\xi t}dt = 2\Gamma(1-\gamma)\cos(\pi(1-\gamma)/2)|\xi|^{\gamma-1} = 2\Gamma(1-\gamma)\sin(\pi\gamma/2)|\xi|^{\gamma-1}$. Correct.
- **Factorization of the symbol (eq 9).** $|\xi|^{\gamma-1} = |\xi|^{-2\beta} = (i\xi)^{-\beta}(-i\xi)^{-\beta}$ with $\beta=(1-\gamma)/2$, principal branches. Time-domain assignment $C_+ = c_\gamma^{1/2}I_+^\beta$ (causal, kernel on $\{s\le t\}$) and $C_- = c_\gamma^{1/2}I_-^\beta$ (anticausal) is standard (Samko–Kilbas–Marichev §5.4). Inverses are Marchaud $D_\pm^\beta$. ✓
- **Adapted FOC (eq 8).** Correctly derived by testing Gâteaux derivative of (1) against adapted variations $\delta u$: $\mathbb E\int \delta u_t(\alpha_t - (Cu)(t))\,dt = 0$ for all adapted $\delta u$ implies, via the optional-projection duality $\mathbb E\int \delta u_t X_t\,dt = \mathbb E\int \delta u_t \,\mathbb E_t[X_t]\,dt$, that $\mathbb E_t[(Cu^\star)(t)] = \alpha_t$ (using $\alpha_t\in\mathcal F_t$).
- **Forecast-curve step $\zeta_s = (D_-^\beta\bar\alpha(s,\cdot))(s)$ (eq 13).** Conditional Fubini (Klenke Thm 14.16) applied to the Marchaud integral $\int_0^\infty (\alpha_{s+h}-\alpha_s)/h^{1+\beta}dh$ is legitimate given the spectral hypothesis; integrand's $L^1$ conditional integrability is inherited from the $\dot H^{1-\gamma}$ bound. Modulo concern 3 (boundedness terminology), the invocation of ref 22 Prop 2.6.13 for "conditional expectation commutes with a linear operator" is standard.
- **Riesz-derivative framing (§1.4, §2.6).** The paper is explicit and consistent: $\mathbb D^{1-\gamma}$ is defined, identified as non-causal, and rejected; only Marchaud one-sided $D_\pm^\beta$ appear in the final formulas. No residual Riesz usage detected in the operator manipulations of §5. ✓
- **OU example (§2.7, eqs 14–15).** $\bar\alpha(t,s) = e^{-\theta(s-t)}\alpha_t$ for $s>t$. Marchaud: $(D_-^\beta e^{-\theta(\cdot-t)})(t) = \theta^\beta$ follows from the identity $D_-^\beta e^{-\theta(\cdot-s)}(s) = \theta^\beta e^{0} = \theta^\beta$ for $\theta>0$ (Samko-Kilbas-Marichev §5.4 example). ✓
- **Markowitz correspondence table (§3).** All eight rows are structurally sound. The "adapted Cholesky" reading of $C=C_-C_+ = C_+^\ast C_+$ is legitimate.

## Summary

No mathematical blockers. The main substantive concerns are (i) §4.1's mis-identification of the constraint-adjoint basis (mixing $\mathbf 1_{[0,T]}$ and $(T-t)\mathbf 1_{[0,T]}$ under a single indicator), and (ii) §4.1's claim that $D_\pm^\beta \mathbf 1_{[0,T]}$ directly yields the Söhngen–Tricomi modes, which conflates the whole-line Marchaud action (exponent $2\beta$) with the finite-interval Fredholm nullspace (exponent $\beta$). Both are exposition/scope issues in the discussion section; the whole-line theorems (§2, §5) are unaffected. Minor Sobolev-domain qualifications on "bounded" statements in §2.6 Step 2 and §5 Step (c) would tighten the operator claims.
