# Research: Adapted Convex Optimization and Physics

## Summary
The "adapted convex duality" skeleton — minimize a convex functional on a Hilbert space subject to a nest / filtration / causal constraint, solve via factorization (Cholesky / Wiener–Hopf / outer / Kolmogorov–Szegő) inside the nest algebra — appears in physics in at least eight largely independent literatures. Causal linear-response theory (Kramers–Kronig, Titchmarsh, Toll), the Wiener–Hopf origins in radiative transfer and diffraction, quantum filtering (Belavkin / Bouten–van Handel–James), path-integral stochastic control (Kappen, Todorov, Theodorou), maximum-caliber and stochastic thermodynamics, JKO/Wasserstein gradient flow with its adapted/causal extensions, and Freidlin–Wentzell / macroscopic fluctuation theory all instantiate one or more of the structural pieces. Only a few sources (Bouten–van Handel–James 2007; Gupta & Hota 2015; the Hardy-space / non-Markovian-kernel arXiv 2604.17058) make the cross-link to filtering theory and Wiener–Hopf factorization explicitly; most physics texts use the structure without naming it.

## Findings

### Cluster 1 — Causality and dispersion relations

1. **Toll's 1956 theorem is the canonical "causality ⇔ dispersion relation" equivalence.** John S. Toll, "Causality and the Dispersion Relation: Logical Foundations," *Phys. Rev.* 104, 1760 (1956): "rigorous proof is given of the logical equivalence of strict causality ('no output before input') and validity dispersion relation, e.g., relation expressing real part generalized scattering amplitude as an integral involving the imaginary part" — i.e. the response function obeys a Hilbert transform between Re and Im. [APS](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.104.1760), [INSPIRE](https://inspirehep.net/literature/1323558).
2. **Titchmarsh's theorem = the Hardy-space formulation.** "If f is square integrable over the real axis, then any one of the following implies the other two: (1) the Fourier transform is 0 for t<0; (2) f(z) is analytic in the upper half-plane and L²-bounded there; (3) Re f and Im f are Hilbert transforms of each other" — i.e. χ(t) causal ⇔ χ̂(ω) ∈ H²(upper half-plane) ⇔ Kramers–Kronig. [MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/TitchmarshTheorem.html). Discussion of the physics-vs-math statement of "Titchmarsh's theorem" in J. Hoffmann-Jørgensen, *Eur. Phys. J. H* (2014): "Titchmarsh's Theorem ... is a compilation of two well-known theorems in mathematics, the Paley-Wiener theorem and the Marcel Riesz theorem." [EPJ H PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1140/epjh/e2014-50021-1.pdf).
3. **Passive susceptibilities ↔ outer/Herglotz–Nevanlinna functions; explicit link to Wiener–Hopf-style factorization.** Gralak, "Macroscopic equations for non-magnetic and non-locally non-linear ..." *C. R. Physique* 21 (2020): for a passive permittivity, Imω>0 implies "the generalized Kramers–Kronig expression of the permittivity ... [corresponds to] the Herglotz–Nevanlinna representation theorem." [PDF](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/physique/article/CRPHYS_2020__21_4-5_343_0.pdf). Welters, Avniel & Johnson, "Speed-of-light limitations in passive linear media" (arXiv:[math-ph/0404070](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0404070)) develops the spectral theory of passive media via positive Herglotz functions; the underlying outer-function decomposition is exactly the Hardy-space factorization used in the adapted-convex skeleton (unverified that Welters labels it "Wiener–Hopf").
4. **Recent (post-2020) physics work explicitly connecting causality, Hardy-space analyticity and non-Markovian memory kernels.** Yusef-Estrada et al., "Causality from Projection and Hardy-Space Analyticity of Non-Markovian Memory Kernels," arXiv:[2604.17058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17058) (2026): "The Kramers–Kronig (KK) dispersion relations connect the real and imaginary parts of a causal response function through a Hilbert transform. While standard for linear-response theory, their validity for non-Markovian memory kernels — the central object governing reduced dynamics in open quantum systems — [requires Hardy-space projection]." This is the cleanest recent statement that the causal projection P+ onto H²+ is the structural object.
5. **Causality and passivity in metamaterials — modern review.** Gustafsson & Sjöberg / Monticone et al., "Causality and Passivity: from Electromagnetism and Network Theory to Metamaterials," arXiv:[2008.05546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05546) (2020): review of how causality + passivity → analyticity → sum rules / bounds. Hayran, Chen, Monticone, "Spectral Causality and the Scattering of Waves," arXiv:[2012.15796](https://arxiv.gg/abs/2012.15796) (2020). Wang & Sárközy, "Generalization of Kramers–Krönig relations ... power-law media," *Comm. Nonlin. Sci. Numer. Sim.* (2020), [Elsevier](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1007570420304949).

**Fit assessment.** *Excellent.* This cluster is literally the adapted-convex skeleton in disguise: causal χ(t) is the L² projection onto the past; χ̂(ω) outer / Hardy-space; KK = unitary equivalence of the past–subspace projection with the Hilbert transform. The only thing missing from the physics literature is the *convex-optimization* framing — passivity gives a quadratic dissipation form but Toll/Titchmarsh do not phrase it as J(χ) → min on a Hilbert space.

### Cluster 2 — Linear response & fluctuation–dissipation

1. **Kubo 1957 (the foundational paper).** R. Kubo, "Statistical-Mechanical Theory of Irreversible Processes. I," *J. Phys. Soc. Jpn.* 12, 570 (1957): "physical quantities such as complex susceptibility ... and complex conductivity ... are rigorously expressed in terms of time-fluctuation of dynamical variables." [JPSJ](https://journals.jps.jp/doi/abs/10.1143/JPSJ.12.570). Follow-up review: R. Kubo, "The fluctuation–dissipation theorem," *Rep. Prog. Phys.* 29, 255 (1966) [PDF](https://www.zib.de/userpage/donati/stochastics2023/06/references/Kubo1966.pdf).
2. **Callen & Welton 1951** is cited as the prior special case; lecture-note treatment showing the lineage Callen–Welton → Kubo–Tomita → Kubo: [Jagiellonian lecture notes](https://th.if.uj.edu.pl/~gudowska/dydaktyka/kubo.pdf).
3. **Causal structure is built in.** URI lecture notes (Müller), "Linear Response and Equilibrium Dynamics," explicitly lists "Causality property of response function" → "Kramers–Kronig dispersion relations" → "Fluctuation–dissipation theorem" as a single chain [PDF](https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?params=%2Fcontext%2Fnonequilibrium_statistical_physics%2Farticle%2F1008%2Ffilename%2F0%2Ftype%2Fadditional%2F&path_info=Chap09_v1.pdf).
4. **Wikipedia "Kubo formula"** gives the standard θ(t−t′) retarded form making causality and time-ordering manifest [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubo_formula).

**Fit assessment.** *Strong.* FDT is a quadratic identity 2k_B T·Im χ(ω) = ω·S(ω) between two causal/adapted objects. The Hessian K is essentially the equilibrium correlation operator; outer/Cholesky factorization corresponds to the standard "spectral density → causal whitening filter" used in Mori/Zwanzig. Convex-optimization framing absent in original Kubo/Callen–Welton but is precisely the structure of maximum-caliber derivations (cluster 6) and Onsager–Machlup (cluster 4).

### Cluster 3 — Wiener–Hopf origins in physics

1. **Original 1931 motivation = Milne problem (radiative transfer).** *Encyclopedia of Math* "Wiener–Hopf equation": "Equations of this type often appear in problems of mathematical physics, e.g. in the theory of radiative transfer (Milne's problem); in the theory of diffraction (diffraction on a half-plane, the problem of boundary refraction). The first studies of equation (1) are due to N. Wiener and E. Hopf, and deal with a factorization method." [link](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Wiener-Hopf_equation). Hopf's 1932 *MNRAS* note "Remarks on the Schwarzschild–Milne model" is the astrophysics companion [ADS](https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1932MNRAS..92..863H); Milne 1921 [ADS](https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1921MNRAS..81..361M); Hopf's *Mathematical Problems of Radiative Equilibrium* (1934 monograph) [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/mathematicalprob029085mbp).
2. **Modern survey of the Wiener–Hopf technique across mathematical physics.** Lawrie & Abrahams (eds.), *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A* survey "The Wiener–Hopf technique, its generalizations and applications," [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8526176/): "the key step in the Wiener–Hopf procedure is to find K+ and K- (the Wiener–Hopf factors)." Surveys application areas: diffraction, elasticity, finance, queueing.
3. **Sommerfeld half-plane → Wiener–Hopf.** Meister & Speck, "The Sommerfeld half-plane problem revisited I: ... a pair of coupled Wiener–Hopf integral equations" (1980) [Deep Blue handle](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/50169) and Meister, "Sommerfeld Diffraction Problems with First and Second Kind Boundary Conditions," *SIAM J. Math. Anal.* 20 (1989) [SIAM](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0520026).
4. **Neutron transport (Case–Zweifel lineage).** Ganapol & Pomraning, "An application of the Wiener–Hopf method to the one-speed flat-flux problem in a half-space," *Ann. Nucl. Energy* (2004) [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306454903000823). Cassell & Williams, "Green's function of one-group transport equation … by Wiener–Hopf technique," *Astrophys. Space Sci.* (1989) explicitly cites Case & Zweifel, *Linear Transport Theory* (1967) [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00645242).
5. **Operator-theoretic factorization (Krein–Gohberg).** I. Gohberg & I. A. Fel'dman, *Convolution Equations and Projection Methods for Their Solution*, AMS Translations of Math. Monographs 41 (1974) [AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/MMONO/41); Bart–Gohberg–Kaashoek–Ran, *Factorization of Matrix and Operator Functions: The State Space Method* (Birkhäuser, 2008) [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-7643-8268-1). These are the canonical sources for Wiener–Hopf factorization as a structural operator-theoretic problem on the half-line — the same factorization the adapted-convex skeleton uses inside the nest algebra.

**Fit assessment.** *Perfect structural fit, partial conceptual fit.* The Wiener–Hopf 1931 paper is literally factorization of a convolution operator on a half-line, i.e. on a maximal nest of subspaces L²[t,∞). Krein/Gohberg recognized this as factorization in a nest algebra avant la lettre. What is missing from the physics literature is the recognition that this factorization is the constructive solver for a *convex* problem (an extremal problem on H²).

### Cluster 4 — Path integrals & stochastic control in physics

Rev.* 91, 1505 (1953) [APS](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.91.1505); part II 91, 1512 [APS](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.91.1512). Rigorous reformulation: Bach & Dürr, "The Onsager–Machlup function as Lagrangian for the most probable path of a diffusion process," *Comm. Math. 2. **Kappen 2005 — path-integral stochastic control.** H. J. Kappen, "Path integrals and symmetry breaking for optimal control theory," *J. Stat. Mech.* P11011 (2005) [IOP](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-5468/2005/11/P11011). Companion: "Linear Theory for Control of Nonlinear Stochastic Systems," *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 95, 200201 (2005) [APS](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.95.200201), arXiv:[physics/0411119](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/physics/0411119).
3. **Todorov / KL-control.** Kappen, Gomez, Opper, "Optimal control as a graphical model inference problem," shows path-integral control = KL minimization in Todorov's 2007 framework [PDF](https://www.mbfys.ru.nl/staff/v.gomez/klcontrol_rev.pdf). 4. **Theodorou path-integral RL.** E. Theodorou et al., "A Generalized Path Integral Control Approach to Reinforcement Learning," *JMLR* 11 (2010) [PDF](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume11/theodorou10a/theodorou10a.pdf); "Stochastic Differential Dynamic Programming" (ACC 2010) [PDF](https://roboti.us/lab/papers/TheodorouACC10.pdf); Theodorou & Todorov, "...for nonlinear Markov jump diffusion processes" (ACC 2012) [PDF](https://roboti.us/lab/papers/TheodorouACC12.pdf).

**Fit assessment.** *Very strong.* Onsager–Machlup is a convex (quadratic-in-velocity) action on path space; the control input is adapted to the state filtration; HJB is convex-dual to the value function. Linearly-solvable / KL-control makes the convex-duality completely explicit (the Cole–Hopf transform = log-Laplace ↔ convex conjugacy). This is essentially the path-space analogue of the adapted-convex skeleton.

### Cluster 5 — Quantum filtering and continuous measurement

1. **Bouten, van Handel & James 2007 — the canonical exposition.** "An Introduction to Quantum Filtering," *SIAM J. Control Optim.* 46 (2007) [SIAM](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/060651239), arXiv:[math/0601741](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0601741): "We describe the quantum Itô calculus ... Companion lecture notes: van Handel, "Quantum filtering: a reference probability approach," arXiv:[math-ph/0508006](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math-ph/0508006): "These notes are intended as an introduction to noncommutative (quantum) filtering theory ... focusing on the spectral theorem and the conditional expectation as the least squares estimate."
2. **Belavkin originals.** V. P. Belavkin, "Quantum stochastic calculus and quantum nonlinear filtering," *J. Multivar. Anal.* 42 (1992) [Elsevier](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0047259X9290042E), preprint arXiv:[math/0512362](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512362): "the problem of QS nonlinear filtering with respect to nondemolition continuous measurements is investigated." Earlier: "Nondemolition measurements, nonlinear filtering and dynamic programming of quantum stochastic processes" (1988) and "Continuous non-demolition observation, quantum filtering and optimal estimation" arXiv:[quant-ph/0509205](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0509205).
3. **Wiseman & Milburn, *Quantum Measurement and Control* (CUP 2010).** [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/quantum-measurement-and-control/F78F445CD9AF00B10593405E9BAC6B9F). Griffith repo PDF [link](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/bitstreams/8d6edf43-6459-5254-aee2-2d4a980390ff/download).
4. **Explicit Kalman ↔ Belavkin analogy.** Gupta & Hota, "Quantum Belavkin filtering versus classical Kushner–Kallianpur filtering — a comparison" [ebrary](https://ebrary.net/189334/mathematics/quantum_belavkin_filtering_versus_classical_kushner_kallianpur_filtering_comparison): explicit side-by-side comparison. Also their paper extending Belavkin–Kalman to non-commutative output signals: *EPJ Quantum Technology* (2015) [PDF](https://epjquantumtechnology.springeropen.com/track/pdf/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-015-0028-y) — "the problem of generalizing the Belavkin–Kalman filter to the case where the classical measurement signal is replaced by a fully quantum non-commutative output signal."
5. **Stochastic master equation review (recent).** Pellegrini, "On quantum stochastic master equations," *Electron. J. Probab.* 30 (2025) [PDF](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-probability/volume-30/issue-none/On-quantum-stochastic-master-equations/10.1214/25-EJP1322.pdf).

**Fit assessment.** *Excellent.* Quantum filtering is literally adapted least-squares estimation in a non-commutative L² (a vN-algebra GNS-space): the measurement filtration is a nest of commutative subalgebras, the innovations representation is the non-commutative Cholesky factorization. Bouten–van Handel–James draw the Kalman–Belavkin analogy explicitly; Gupta–Hota make it a comparison theorem. **Confirms the user's suspicion**: BvHJ + Gupta–Hota are essentially the only explicit Kalman/Belavkin = single mathematical structure papers I found.

### Cluster 8 — Large deviations, Freidlin–Wentzell, instantons, MFT

1. **Touchette 2009 review.** H. Touchette, "The large deviation approach to statistical mechanics," *Phys. Mod. Phys.* 87, 593 (2015) [APS](https://journals.aps.org/rmp/abstract/10.1103/RevModPhys.87.593); foundational paper arXiv:[cond-mat/0104153](http://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0104153): "a crucial role is played by the time reversed dynamics ... modification of the Onsager–Machlup theory in the SNS, a general Hamilton–Jacobi equation for the macroscopic entropy." **Fit assessment.** *Excellent.* Freidlin–Wentzell and MFT are textbook examples of convex variational problems over adapted (forward-in-time) paths, solved via Hamilton–Jacobi (convex duality) and instanton equations. The "minimum-action path" is computed by a convex optimization with causality automatic. MFT explicitly couples this with time-reversed dynamics — the natural setting for the Cholesky/Wiener–Hopf reverse-time factorization.

### Bonus — Kalman / Wiener–Hopf vs. linear response / quantum measurement as one structure

- The cleanest cross-link source: **Bouten–van Handel–James 2007** (arXiv:math/0601741) — explicitly built on Kalman/Kushner–Kallianpur classical filtering, ported via noncommutative L² and reference probability, with Belavkin's filter as the noncommutative Kalman.
- **Gupta & Hota** ([ebrary](https://ebrary.net/189334/mathematics/quantum_belavkin_filtering_versus_classical_kushner_kallianpur_filtering_comparison); [EPJ QT 2015](https://epjquantumtechnology.springeropen.com/track/pdf/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-015-0028-y)) — explicit comparison and generalization.
- The **Hardy-space / non-Markovian kernel** paper arXiv:[2604.17058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17058) is the only recent source I located that connects linear response (KK) explicitly to the Hardy-space projection structure used in filtering / Wiener–Hopf factorization. This is the closest existing physics paper to the "one mathematical structure" thesis.
- I found **no physics paper** that fuses *all* of (i) Kalman-style adapted estimation, (ii) Kramers–Kronig outer factorization, and (iii) Wiener–Hopf in radiative transfer / diffraction under a single banner. This appears to be a genuinely open synthesis.
