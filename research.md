# Research: Review of `papers/noisy-signal-impact-trading.md`

## Summary
The draft is plausibly useful as a stationary, frequency-domain/Wiener--Hopf pedagogical treatment, but its novelty should be stated very narrowly. Much of the surrounding territory--transient propagators with signals, OU/exponential cases, general/power-law propagators, operator-resolvent/Fredholm solutions, and fractional-derivative power-law liquidation--is already active and quite close; the safest claim is an elementary infinite-horizon causal-filter derivation and the specific AR(1) scalar-collapse/noisy-observation composition, not a new general execution-with-signals framework.

## Findings
1. **Gârleanu--Pedersen is mis-positioned if described as only instantaneous/no-transient costs.** The draft says GP13 has “quadratic instantaneous transaction costs (no transient impact),” but the paper explicitly says its transaction-cost specification is “sufficiently rich to allow for both purely transitory and persistent costs,” and that with persistent costs “price changes due to the trader’s market impact persist for a while.” This does not make GP identical to a Bouchaud/Gatheral propagator model, but the contrast should be softened/corrected. [Gârleanu & Pedersen PDF](https://docs.lhpedersen.com/DynamicTrading.pdf)

2. **The broad “signals + transient impact” space is already heavily occupied.** Lehalle--Neuman incorporate a Markovian/OU signal into the Gatheral--Schied--Slynko transient-impact framework and derive an explicit OU + exponential-resilience strategy, though largely in a finite-horizon/fuel-constraint setting and initially over deterministic strategies; they also note the adaptive-signal version as a challenge. This means the draft’s AR(1)/exponential result should be framed as a different stationary causal-filter analogue, not as the first explicit signal/resilience calculation. [arXiv:1704.00847](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00847)

3. **General propagator + signals has recent operator-resolvent literature that is very close to the draft’s operator language.** Abi Jaber--Neuman (2022) solve optimal liquidation with transient Volterra propagators and progressively measurable signals via infinite-dimensional stochastic control, operator-valued Riccati/BSDE machinery, and explicit optimal strategies; the paper emphasizes implementability for singular kernels such as power laws. This undercuts any broad novelty claim around “general K + signals + Fredholm/Volterra.” [arXiv:2211.00447](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.00447v2)

4. **The 2024/2026 cross-impact propagator paper is even closer to portfolio-choice language.** Abi Jaber--Neuman--Tuschmann solve continuous-time portfolio choice with matrix-valued Volterra propagators, temporary impact, risk, and progressively measurable alpha signals; their solution is given explicitly via resolvents after reducing FOCs to stochastic Fredholm equations, and they discuss power-law kernels and alpha-decay effects. The draft should distinguish itself by infinite-horizon stationarity, scalar convolution, and spectral/Wiener--Hopf factorization--not by “operator resolvent/Fredholm with signals.” [arXiv:2403.10273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10273)

5. **Power-law/fractional-derivative claims are plausible but not novel in broad form.** Forde/Sánchez-Betancourt et al. characterize signal-adaptive liquidation under power-law resilience and Gaussian signals, including OU and fBm, and state that the resulting Fredholm equations “can be solved in terms of fractional derivatives.” The draft’s stationary statement “power-law kernel -> causal fractional derivative of order `(1-beta)/2`” may be a clean asymptotic/spectral version, but related-work should cite this as a very close precedent and avoid implying that the fractional-operator connection is new. [Oxford ORA record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:0c794b99-5276-48e4-90d7-60a127082c26)

6. **The admissible power-law range needs qualification.** The draft often writes power-law kernels with `0 < beta < 1`. In finite-horizon Volterra `L^2` frameworks, singular kernels `t^{-beta}` are typically admitted only for `0 < beta < 1/2`; the cross-impact paper states this explicitly, while softened kernels `(1+t/t0)^(-beta)` can allow `beta in (0,1)`. The draft can keep `0<beta<1` only if it clearly stays in a stationary/distributional spectral setting and handles the zero-frequency singularity/regularization. [Cross-impact propagators, Example 2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10273)

7. **No-dynamic-arbitrage/positive-definiteness should be stated more carefully.** For a linear transient-impact quadratic cost, nonnegative definiteness of the symmetrized kernel is the right convexity/no-round-trip condition. But Gatheral’s no-dynamic-arbitrage paper is broader: it links instantaneous impact shape `f` and decay `G`, shows exponential decay is compatible only with linear instantaneous impact, and derives power-law constraints such as `gamma + delta >= 1` for power-law impact/decay. The draft’s sentence “`Khat>0` is equivalent to absence of dynamic arbitrage in the sense of Gatheral” is acceptable only for the linearized/symmetric quadratic submodel, not for the full Gatheral setup. [Gatheral PDF](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/3683/ecb4dae470f4df219d65bb6491d3c7fd4b78.pdf)

8. **The Bouchaud propagator citation is appropriate but the draft uses only a linearized subcase.** Bouchaud--Gefen--Potters--Wyart introduce/study a propagator model where price is the cumulative impact of past trades through a nonconstant time propagator, motivated by long-memory order flow and empirical power-law response. However their empirical model is not simply the draft’s linear symmetric quadratic cost; it includes trade-sign/order-flow structure and often concave instantaneous impact. The draft should say it borrows the propagator/resilience idea, not the full BGPW microstructure model. [arXiv:cond-mat/0307332](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0307332)

9. **Noisy-signal separation is plausible but should be framed as classical certainty equivalence, not a finance novelty unless carefully scoped.** The draft’s “denoise by causal Wiener filter, then apply the causal impact rule” is the standard separation principle for linear-quadratic-Gaussian/partial-observation control: optimal estimation and control separate, with the Kalman/Wiener estimate fed into the full-information controller. It may still be useful to spell this out for stationary propagator execution, but the novelty claim should be limited to this particular spectral execution formulation. [Berkeley LQG notes](https://berkeley-me233.github.io/static/ME233_Sp16_L8_LQG.pdf)

10. **The new 2025 nonlinear propagator-with-alpha paper further narrows novelty.** Abi Jaber et al. formulate optimal trading with alpha signals and nonlinear transient propagator impact, including power-law decay, deriving nonlinear stochastic Fredholm equations and a convergent iterative scheme. This is not the same as the draft’s linear stationary Wiener--Hopf calculation, but it is a recent sign that “alpha + general propagator + Fredholm” is no longer an open niche. [arXiv:2503.04323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04323)

11. **The draft should avoid saying Wiener--Hopf appears in GSS/AN22 unless it verifies exact use.** GSS12 is centered on finite-horizon transient linear impact and Fredholm integral equations; AN22/AJN24 use stochastic Fredholm equations, Riccati equations, and operator resolvents. These are related operator equations, but not necessarily the stationary spectral Wiener--Hopf factorization in equation (6). Safer wording: “finite-horizon analogues appear as Fredholm/resolvent equations; the stationary convolution case can be diagonalized and factorized by Wiener--Hopf.” [GSS SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1531466), [arXiv:2211.00447](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.00447v2)

12. **Best defensible novelty statement.** A plausible narrow positioning is: “We give a self-contained stationary convolution/filter derivation of causal optimal trading with linear transient impact; in the AR(1) + exponential kernel case the Wiener--Hopf anticausal projection collapses to an explicit scalar, yielding a simple kernel-innovation rule; with additive observation noise, classical Wiener/LQG separation composes the estimator with that stationary execution filter.” This is credible if the paper explicitly contrasts finite-horizon liquidation/portfolio-choice results and avoids claiming novelty for propagators, signals, Fredholm equations, power-law fractional derivatives, or separation principle in general.
